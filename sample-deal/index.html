The price where this BRRR works, and why you probably won't get it

This is a real house I appraised in October 2026, with the address taken out. It's still on the market and it isn't a deal on offer. I've worked it at the price where it meets a typical BRRR target: most of your money back out on refinance, and the rest paid back from the rent in under 4 years. That price is 31% below asking, and the section below explains why a vendor is unlikely to accept it.

3-bed semi-detached house, 64 sqm, needing a light refurbishment. Asking £195,000, on the market for 43 days with no price cut when I appraised it. Inside Nottingham City, where selective licensing covers most rented homes.

Why £135,000 is unlikely

I'd rather you hear this from me than find it out after a month of offers.

It's 31% below asking, and nothing says the vendor needs to sell. The house had been listed for 43 days with no price cut. There's no probate, no chain to protect and no auction deadline. My working rule is that without a reason to sell quickly, a vendor will usually take no more than about 10% off. Here that's around £175,500, a long way above £135,000.

The work is light. Big discounts usually come with houses most buyers can't or won't take on: no kitchen, damp, a roof, an extension that needs signing off. A house that needs decorating, flooring and a bathroom attracts first-time buyers and other landlords, and they'll pay close to the asking price.

One sale on the same road is low. A slightly bigger house on the same road sold for £182,000 in March 2026. If the lender's valuer leans on that sale rather than the others, the valuation falls and more of your money stays in. The 10% lower valuation test below shows what that does.

What would change it: the house sitting unsold for months, a price cut, a vendor who needs a quick sale, or a survey finding more work than the listing suggests. Any of these can open up a much bigger discount. Until then, £135,000 is the number I'd work towards, not one I'd expect.

What each price leaves in the deal

The rent isn't the problem here. The valuation is: the mortgage is capped at 75% of £214,500, which is £160,875, so every pound the purchase and costs come to above that stays in the deal.

What I'd want before committing

  • An accepted offer at or near £135,000, which as explained above is unlikely without a change in the vendor's position
  • Two agent valuations, and the condition of the £182,000 same-road sale
  • A builder's itemised quote after a viewing, including the boiler and electrics
  • A letting agent's rent figure for this house, and the licensing position from the council
  • Tenure from the title register, and confirmation it's standard brick construction

Main assumptions

  • Stamp duty at standard rates plus the 5% surcharge
  • Holding costs £500 a month until let
  • Bridging: 75% of the price, 0.85% a month, 2% arrangement, 1% exit
  • Buy-to-let: 75% loan to value, 5.75% 5-year fix, rent must cover interest by 125%, £3,500 refinance costs
  • Letting: 10% management, 2 weeks' void a year, £1,650 a year other costs

All figures are before tax. On a live deal I'll re-run the numbers on your own assumptions.
